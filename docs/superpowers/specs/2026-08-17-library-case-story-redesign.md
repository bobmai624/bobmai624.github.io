# Library Evaluation Case Story Redesign

## Intent

Replace the sequential PDF-page gallery with a legible usability-research case study. The page should explain the evaluation journey, show a small set of equally weighted evidence crops, translate findings into eight recommendations, and present the video at a full 16:9 reading width.

## Content hierarchy

1. A sharp split cover pairs a native 1280×720 lab-session frame with code-native typography and study counts.
2. A four-step research journey explains how the team moved from first impression to moderated tasks, gaze/behaviour evidence, and prioritised design action.
3. Three findings combine bounded participant counts with landscape evidence crops. PDF pages are never used as full-page decoration.
4. All eight recommendations remain visible in two prioritised groups and retain their finding linkage.
5. The local presentation video receives its own uninterrupted 16:9 stage, comparable to the Vita walkthrough.
6. The original report and video remain downloadable in the source archive.

## Evidence boundaries

- The five-second test involved 14 participants; the moderated lab evaluation involved 5 participants and 5 tasks.
- Claims remain explicitly sample-bounded. The page must not imply population-level validation.
- Eye-tracking is presented as supporting evidence for observed visibility issues, not as proof of causality.
- Source screenshots are shown at or below their useful native detail. Enlarged low-resolution crops are not used as the cover.

## Responsive behaviour

Desktop uses a two-column editorial rhythm, landscape evidence cards, and a full-width video. Tablet and mobile collapse to one column while preserving the research sequence, media aspect ratios, captions, language switching, and source links.
